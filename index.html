What Is namvis?

namvis stands for Next-Scale Autoregressive Multi-View Image Synthesis. Behind that long name is a simple idea: give the program a small set of photos of an object or scene, and it builds brand-new viewpoints that were never photographed. In everyday terms, it lets you walk around something you only photographed from the front.

The software was introduced in a research paper accepted at NeurIPS 2026, a well-known conference in the machine learning community. This desktop release packages that research into a point-and-click application so that photographers, designers, students, hobbyists, and curious tinkerers can use it without touching a single line of code.

Unlike traditional tools that demand dozens or even hundreds of photographs from every possible angle, namvis is designed for sparse input. A handful of well-chosen pictures is enough to produce a convincing result. That makes it practical for real life, where you rarely have a studio rig and a turntable at your disposal.

✨ What You Can Do With namvis

The application is built around a single, satisfying workflow: load your photos, generate, and explore. Here are some of the things people use it for.

  • See an object from new angles. Photograph a sculpture, a shoe, a toy, or a piece of furniture from three or four sides and let namvis fill in the missing views.
  • Build a turntable-style preview. Generate a smooth sweep of viewpoints around your subject, perfect for product pages, portfolios, and presentations.
  • Bring old photos back to life. Turn a small set of snapshots from a trip into a set of views that feels like you were standing there.
  • Explore ideas quickly. Designers can mock up a concept, photograph it roughly, and preview how it reads from multiple directions before committing to a final render.
  • Learn how modern image generation works. Students and enthusiasts can experiment with an autoregressive model that predicts images scale by scale, starting with a rough outline and refining it into detail.

Because the underlying method builds images in stages rather than all at once, results tend to stay coherent across viewpoints. Shapes, textures, and lighting are carried consistently from one view to the next instead of drifting apart.

Most problems have simple causes. Work through these suggestions in order and you will usually be back on track within a few minutes.

  • The download stopped partway. Delete the incomplete file and start again from the same page. Interrupted downloads are common on unstable connections.
  • Windows shows a warning when you open the file. This is normal for software downloaded from the internet. Confirm that you want to run it, and only download from the official link on this page.

  • Generation feels very slow. Close other heavy applications while namvis runs. Large image sets and long viewpoint sweeps naturally take longer than a single quick preview.
